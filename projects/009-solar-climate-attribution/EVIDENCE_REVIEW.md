# Evidence review v0.1

Date: 2026-09-05. Status: provisional literature assessment. Bibliographic details and access limits are in [sources.json](sources.json); a readable catalogue can be generated with `validate.py --catalogue build/project-009/SOURCES.md`.

## 1. State the claims before testing them

The circulated argument joins several different propositions: proxy records contain long-period structure; that structure originates in the Sun; its phase and amplitude remain predictable; it governs global temperature; it dominates recent warming; and it predicts a dated return to cooling. Evidence for an earlier proposition is not sufficient evidence for every later one. The separate judgements are recorded in [claims.json](claims.json).

The question is not whether natural climate change exists. It is whether a proposed explanation accounts for the magnitude, timing and geographical and vertical pattern of the observations, with an uncertainty budget and credible alternatives included.

## 2. What the three supplied graphics actually contain

This is a visual/caption audit of screenshots supplied by the project initiator. Original post permalinks, full plotting code and numerical figure inputs have not been obtained; independent provenance and reproduction remain open.

**Composite Bray/Eddy graphic:** the caption describes filtered radiocarbon-related frequencies and an extrapolation after 1950. The vertical axis says warmer/cooler without a temperature scale. The smooth composite is not presented with a temperature calibration, uncertainty bands or residuals against global observations. An apparent agreement between its labelled peaks and historical epochs does not establish quantitative temperature skill.

**Carbon-14/iceberg graphic:** the caption identifies a carbon-14-related curve, a North Atlantic drift-ice proxy, a millennial sine curve and selected historical events. These are different kinds of evidence and must not be combined into an implied measured global-temperature series. We have not independently checked every plotted point, sign, unit or historical bar.

**Grand-minima graphic:** the caption calls the sine curves a proposed time evolution, and says its highlighted windows occupy 54% of the Holocene. That broad coverage makes an explicit null model essential. No binomial significance calculation is justified from the screenshot alone: minima have durations, dates have uncertainty, events can cluster, and periods, phases and the window threshold may have been selected using the same events. We need the full event list and selection procedure before testing alignment.

These are criticisms of what the figures establish, not an allegation that their underlying observations were fabricated.

## 3. Proxies are genuine evidence, but require interpretation

Radiocarbon calibration is a measurement-based scientific enterprise. IntCal13 supplies calibration information, not a global temperature curve [S09]. Cosmogenic records require models separating solar modulation, geomagnetic shielding and archive processes; they are not direct readings of watts per square metre or degrees Celsius [S02, S10].

Stopping a natural radiocarbon analysis near 1950 is not itself suspicious: nuclear testing and fossil-carbon effects complicate the modern record. Nilsson and colleagues explicitly discuss this [S03]. The problem is using an extrapolated cycle as if it validates what happened subsequently, when independent instrumental observations should instead be used to test it.

## 4. Eddy periodicity: do not cherry-pick either side

Steinhilber et al. (2012) reported a significant approximately 1,000-year periodicity in their reconstructed solar activity, and compared solar variability with an Asian monsoon-related proxy [S05]. That is real favourable evidence, not a non-existent literature. It does not quantify a dominant solar contribution to modern global warming.

Usoskin's 2023 review, Section 4.1, finds the millennial variations statistically insignificant in the reconstruction and noise comparison it discusses [S02]. The appropriate assessment is sensitivity to reconstruction, time interval and method, not a categorical assertion that nobody has detected such a signal. Neither result establishes a fixed-phase clock suitable for century-ahead temperature prediction.

## 5. Bray/Hallstatt: a substantive disagreement about origin

The labels are often paired in public discussion, but an approximately 2,400-year spectral feature is not automatically identical to every proposed 2,500-year climate oscillator.

Usoskin et al. (2016) interpret a shared approximately 2,400-year component in reconstructed carbon-14 and beryllium-10 series as solar [S06]. Their 2023 review nevertheless notes limited robustness given the small number of cycles [S02]. Nilsson et al. (2024) instead explain the millennial component through geomagnetic variations after accounting for hemispherical production differences [S03].

Schanner et al. (2025) jointly reconstruct geomagnetic and solar influences with a flexible Bayesian model. Allowing an explicit long-term solar component does not make that component necessary to explain their data [S04]. This is evidence against the proposed interpretation, not proof that all long-term solar variation is impossible. The 2024 and 2025 studies share authors and methodological lineage: they are not two wholly independent replications.

A fair replication must compare assumptions and shared inputs, including geomagnetic priors, isotope production, chronologies, carbon-cycle assumptions and preprocessing. Counting papers is not a substitute for that exercise.

## 6. Historical warmth is not a globally synchronous temperature clock

Bond et al. (2001) reported North Atlantic proxy correlations and proposed solar influence and potential amplification [S08]. Its geographical scope and inferred mechanism should be retained. Neukom et al. (2019) found that preindustrial warm and cold epochs did not have the globally coherent timing implied by a single smoothly labelled line [S12].

Regional warmth, vineyards, settlement and glacier advances can inform historical climate. They do not supply a quantitative global mean without geographical sampling, calibration and uncertainty. Social outcomes also require non-climatic explanations to be considered. Historical data can test causal models; it would be wrong to say such data cannot exist or cannot contribute to attribution.

## 7. The modern attribution test

NASA's FAQ distinguishes historical solar reconstruction from satellite monitoring since 1978 and reports no upward trend in incoming solar energy over the satellite record [S01]. This is an important constraint on an explanation based on increasing irradiance, not a complete stand-alone test of every possible delayed or indirect mechanism.

The IPCC explicitly assesses solar forcing, contradicting the claim that it ignores the Sun. AR6 Chapter 7 gives a solar-cycle-averaged best estimate of solar effective radiative forcing of 0.01 W/m², with a likely range of -0.06 to +0.08 W/m², using its stated preindustrial and modern cycle conventions. Total anthropogenic forcing over 1750–2019 is 2.72 W/m², with a different uncertainty convention [S15]. These are published assessments, not Project 009 calculations; they should not be divided into a simplistic attribution percentage.

A model must also explain ocean heat storage, the atmospheric temperature profile and top-of-atmosphere energy changes. Flat forcing can coexist with a lagged temperature response, so response times and initial conditions must be explicitly tested. The current evidence does not support replacing human attribution with an uncalibrated sine-wave extrapolation [S01, S15].

## 8. Assess the author's strongest actual argument

Vinós's book abstract proposes solar modulation of poleward energy transport. Its stated themes also include orbital cycles, abrupt glacial changes, tides, Holocene variability, volcanism and greenhouse gases [S13]. The full book has not been obtained for page-level assessment in this project.

In the author's public Q&A with Andy May, question 6 explicitly does not rule out anthropogenic warming; the smaller human contribution is conditional on their hypothesis being correct. The same Q&A describes a dynamical rather than simply increased-total-irradiance mechanism [S14]. Thus the categorical public claim that the Sun, not humans, is responsible is stronger than this author explanation.

Solar UV, ozone and circulation pathways are already topics of research, as illustrated by Gray et al. (2010); its figure correction must accompany reuse [S10, S11]. A lack of a TSI trend alone does not disprove every heat-transport hypothesis. The proposed chain needs quantified links from the solar perturbation to transport, outgoing energy, heat storage and temperature, with prediction independent of calibration.

## 9. Forecasting and statistical safeguards

A fitted periodic model is a hypothesis. Filtering near a chosen frequency can make a smooth oscillation visible even when its forecasting skill has not been demonstrated. Turner et al. (2016) give a relevant example of solar-like periodicities in stochastic simulations; their peatland analysis is a methodological warning, not a universal refutation of solar cycles [S16].

We also retain a newer solar-oriented regression paper, Stefani (2026), in the register rather than excluding it because its conclusion is inconvenient. Only its abstract and publication metadata have been checked. Its coefficient selection, endpoint sensitivity, uncertainty and forecast design require full review before evidential use [S19].

The claim of another century of melting followed by solar cooling is not validated by the supplied figures. A glacier forecast needs temperature and precipitation pathways, regional geometry and response times as well as any solar scenario. This project has not run a glacier model or a long-term forecast.

## 10. Working conclusion and revision conditions

The figures do not establish the categorical solar-only attribution or a reliable dated cooling forecast. That conclusion is compatible with genuine solar variability, useful proxy evidence and legitimate research into indirect mechanisms.

We would revise the assessment in response to an independently reproducible, uncertainty-aware solar mechanism that explains modern observations and improves held-out prediction over appropriately specified alternatives. A failure of one particular solar-cycle model would not disprove all solar influence. Equally, a newly detected cycle would not on its own establish modern warming attribution.
