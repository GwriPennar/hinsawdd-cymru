# Official evidence audit for Project 002

**Audit date:** 2 August 2026

This is the source-decision baseline for later official-projection work. It is not a comprehensive history of climate policy.

## Wales

### Climate Adaptation Strategy for Wales 2024

The current published national adaptation strategy was issued on 22 October 2024. It describes existing and planned adaptation activity across government.

Official source:

- https://www.gov.wales/climate-adaptation-strategy-wales-2024

Project 002 interpretation:

- it is the current published national policy baseline identified by this audit;
- it is primarily an adaptation strategy, not a newly generated Wales temperature-projection ensemble;
- its current status and any subsequent updates should be assessed through official Welsh Government publications, formal monitoring reports and relevant Senedd proceedings.

### Climate Change Committee assessment of Wales

The CCC published `Adapting to climate change: Progress in Wales` on 14 September 2023. It found positive planning examples but insufficient delivery and implementation, limited monitoring and substantial indicator-data gaps.

Official source:

- https://www.theccc.org.uk/publication/adapting-to-climate-change-progress-in-wales/

Project 002 interpretation:

- the 2023 assessment is the latest dedicated CCC Wales adaptation-progress report identified in this audit;
- the 2024 strategy responded to that evidence and policy context;
- a later evidence review should establish whether an updated monitoring or projection timetable has since been published.

### Welsh evidence-review boundary

Project 002 may use official Welsh Government publications and formal Senedd records to establish technical assumptions, update cycles, monitoring arrangements and public accountability for scientific evidence.

This review does not assess political parties, manifestos, electoral positions or political performance. Senedd material is relevant only where it records official evidence, formal scrutiny, ministerial statements or published commitments concerning the scientific and monitoring framework.

## United Kingdom

### UK Climate Projections 2018

UKCP18 remains the operational national projection service and its user interface continued to receive releases in 2026.

Official sources:

- https://ukclimateprojections-ui.metoffice.gov.uk/help/guidance
- https://ukclimateprojections-ui.metoffice.gov.uk/ui/releases

Project 002 interpretation:

- UKCP18 offers probabilistic, global, regional, local and derived products with different appropriate uses;
- the official projection stage must select a product based on temporal resolution, Wales spatial aggregation, ensemble information and documented limitations;
- choosing a single UK-wide headline number would be inadequate for the proposed Wales model.

### UK Climate Information

The Met Office began introducing UK Climate Information in summer 2026 as the next generation of national climate projections, building on UKCP18. The service is still developing.

Official sources:

- https://weather.metoffice.gov.uk/climate/uk-climate/uk-climate-information-ukci
- https://www.metoffice.gov.uk/research/approach/collaboration/ukcp/news

Project 002 interpretation:

- the Stage B source decision should be revisited as UKCI datasets and technical documentation mature;
- UKCP18 should not be called invalid merely because it was originally released in 2018;
- it is nonetheless reasonable to test its historical range against the expanded observations and to document the transition to UKCI.

### Fourth Independent Assessment of UK Climate Risk

The CCC published `A Well-Adapted UK` on 20 May 2026 as its independent advice for the fourth UK Climate Change Risk Assessment. It advises preparing at minimum for hazards associated with around 2°C global warming by 2050, while recognising a high-end possibility around 4°C by 2100.

Official source:

- https://www.theccc.org.uk/publication/a-well-adapted-uk/

Project 002 interpretation:

- this is a risk and adaptation assessment, not a Wales annual-temperature forecast series;
- it provides current policy-relevant warming-level framing for later scenario comparison;
- its technical report assesses risks separately across England, Northern Ireland, Scotland and Wales, but evidence is uneven between nations and sectors.

## Europe

### European Climate Risk Assessment

The European Environment Agency published the first European Climate Risk Assessment in 2024. It identifies 36 major climate risks. A second assessment is planned for 2028.

Official sources:

- https://climate-adapt.eea.europa.eu/en/eu-adaptation-policy/key-eu-actions/european-climate-risk-assessment/
- https://www.eea.europa.eu/en/analysis/publications/european-climate-risk-assessment/european-climate-risk-assessment

Project 002 interpretation:

- EUCRA is useful for cross-border and systemic risk context;
- it is not the preferred direct numerical input for a Wales mean-temperature pathway;
- the 2025 European State of the Climate provides a newer observation-based European update but is also not a Wales projection ensemble.

Copernicus source:

- https://climate.copernicus.eu/esotc/2025

## Global

### Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change

The latest completed full IPCC assessment remains the Sixth Assessment Report, whose synthesis report was finalised in March 2023. The IPCC is now in its seventh assessment cycle.

Official sources:

- https://www.ipcc.ch/synthesis-report/
- https://www.ipcc.ch/assessment-report/ar6/

Project 002 interpretation:

- AR6 supplies the global physical-science and scenario framework;
- it should not be converted directly into a single Wales local-temperature line;
- official UK regional or local projections are more appropriate for the Stage C numerical model because they are designed for UK adaptation analysis.

## Current source decision

For Stage A, Project 002 uses no external future-projection dataset. It publishes only a transparent regression baseline from independently verified Project 001 observations.

For Stage B, UKCP18 remains the current operational candidate source family, while UKCI is watched as the emerging replacement. No official-ensemble implementation should begin until the exact UKCP or UKCI product, baseline, scenarios, spatial aggregation and licence are documented in a source-decision record.
